# Watchability design references for an observation-first ant colony simulation (soil cross-section, colony acts on its own)

Research date: 12 October 2026. View counts, review counts and subscriber numbers below are snapshots from the dates given and will have moved since. Third-party trackers (Social Blade, vidIQ, SteamPulse, Steambase, aggregators) are flagged as such.

## 1. Why people watch ant farms, formicaria and ant-keeping channels, and which moments draw views or comments

### Takeaway
The biggest ant-keeping channel, AntsCanada, gets its views by turning colonies into named characters with ongoing storylines: kingdoms, wars, plagues, queen deaths, relocations. The narration is melodramatic, and viewers get to vote on decisions. The best-performing titles are about conflict (fire ants vs. hand, ant war, ants vs. tarantula), disaster ("Coming Back Home to Disaster") and reveals ("Showed Me THIS in their New ANT FARM"). The hobbyist community pushes back on conflict that is staged for views. I found no hard engagement data broken down by moment type, only view counts per video.

**Community criticism (dramatisation vs. realism)**
- Formiculture forum users describe AntsCanada's audience as mostly people "who know nothing about antkeeping" and call him "an entertainer, not an antkeeper". This is one community's opinion. — [Formiculture](https://www.formiculture.com/topic/17459-a-new-low-for-ac/page-2)
- A 2021 Formiculture thread, "A new low for AC", criticises making colonies fight for views. Commenters called it cruel and predicted that most of the colonies would die. — [Formiculture "A new low for AC"](https://www.formiculture.com/topic/17459-a-new-low-for-ac)

**Livestreams**
- Ant livestreams are small hobbyist efforts. Examples: a 2023 "Antcam" of a *Lasius niger* colony that went live automatically every day in daylight; a 2015 live feed of pavement ants trying to pull out and eat a mealworm; a 2017 YouTube stream of an ant colony collection. — [Formiculture Antcam](https://formiculture.com/topic/20604-antcamlive-antcamlive-yes-its-working-come-see-and-chat); [Arachnoboards](https://arachnoboards.com/threads/live-stream-feed.286385); [Formiculture 2017 live](https://formiculture.com/topic/4905-live-the-greatest-ant-colony-collection-ever)

### Inferences
- In real ant media, the content that gets watched has an authored layer on top of raw footage: names for colonies and queens, a narrator who attributes motive ("aggressive and lethal"), stakes framed as cliffhangers, and chapter-like events. Raw continuous footage (the livestreams) shows no sign of large audiences. For a sim where the player watches, this points to a narration or chronicle layer on top of the simulation, for example auto-naming the colony and queen, plus event headlines such as "The queen has died" or "Flood in the lower galleries".
- The recurring high-view topics map onto events a sim can generate: first chambers and a new nest ("new ant farm"), predators entering the colony, wars with neighbouring colonies, disasters (mites, floods, losses while the keeper was away), queen death or succession, and feeding time-lapses.
- The hobbyist backlash against staged fights suggests that realism-minded viewers dislike conflict forced for spectacle. A realistic sim may hold this audience better if conflict comes out of the simulation rather than being scripted.

### Gaps
- No engagement data (comments, retention) broken down by moment type for AntsCanada or other creators. Only view counts for a few titles, from scraped sources.
- Not researched: Ant Lab, other YouTube/TikTok ant creators and AntsCanada's "ant kingdom" playlists. I found no stats for them.
- I found no 24/7 ant colony livestream with a meaningful audience, and no audience figures for the hobby streams above.
- The content of the TEDx talk was not retrieved.

## 2. Digital ant sims and games: underground worlds, fog of war, exploration, map variety, soil, other creatures, threats, reception

### Takeaway
SimAnt (1991) already had the design this sim needs: a top-down surface view, a side-view nest, rival colonies, predators, human hazards, and rain that washes away trails and floods nests. Its key idea was intelligence distributed through pheromones. Empires of the Undergrowth (95% positive across about 10.9K Steam reviews) shows that a documentary narrator and nests that look realistic are well liked. Its weak points were short content and the lack of save/pause. Mobile ant games are mostly free-to-play strategy with nest upgrades. A new idle ant game, *Ant* (Fruitdish, planned for 2027), uses layered excavation (cabinet, pipes, soil, roots, clay, stone, groundwater) as its progression.

### Cited Findings
**SimAnt (Maxis, Will Wright and Justin McCormick, November 1991)**
- Views: a top-down yard view for the surface, a side view for the ant nests, and an overhead map of the yard and house in the Full Game. Modes: Quick Game (black vs. red colony in one patch of yard), Full Game (spread across the yard into the house by producing young queens and drones; you win by eliminating the red colonies and driving out the humans), and Experimental mode (place pheromone trails, walls, rocks, food and pesticide; control red ants and spiders). — [Wikipedia: SimAnt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SimAnt)
- Threats: rival red ants; spiders, antlions and caterpillars (groups of ants can kill them); human footsteps, electrical outlets, bug spray and lawnmowers; rain, which washes away pheromone trails and can flood nests. — [Wikipedia: SimAnt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SimAnt)
- The SNES version added eight scenarios, each set in a different locale with its own hazards. The boxed game included a manual with detailed information about ants and ant societies. — [Wikipedia: SimAnt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SimAnt)
- Reception: *Dragon* #178 (1992) gave it 5/5. *Computer Gaming World* (Mar 1992) said strategy players would "hit paydirt". GamePro named it "Educational Game of the Year" (Feb 1994). Sales were middling, with over 50,000 copies by Feb 1992. — [Wikipedia: SimAnt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SimAnt)
- Wright, as reported via his AV Club interview, said the ants used pheromone trails, which led him to the idea of distributing intelligence "not through the agents, but through the environment". He called SimAnt probably the biggest inspiration for The Sims. He wanted to convince adults that ants were interesting, but the core audience turned out to be about twelve-year-old boys. — [Jesper Juul's blog on the AV Club interview](https://www.jesperjuul.net/ludologist/?p=155); [Wikipedia: Will Wright](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Will_Wright_(game_designer))

**Empires of the Undergrowth (Slug Disco; Early Access 1 Dec 2017, full release 7 Jun 2024)**
- Steam: "Overwhelmingly Positive", 95% of 10,877 English reviews; recent reviews 94% of 139 (snapshot Oct 2026). — [Steam store page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/463530/Empires_of_the_Undergrowth/)
- Framing: a narrative campaign that pairs a lab experiment with "a documentary series that explores the various species of ants in their natural biomes". The nest grows from a central queen chamber, and workers dig "through a hex-based maze of soil and bedrock" to build storerooms, nurseries and hallways. There are "multiple underground and overground maps". Food sources are seeds, nectar, fungus, honeydew, insects and amphibians. Enemies include ladybugs, beetles (devil's coach horse), arachnids, amphibians, crustaceans, hermit crabs and nocturnal predators. Modes are the campaign, AI skirmishes and seasonal challenges. The store page mentions no procedural or endless mode. — [Steam store page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/463530/Empires_of_the_Undergrowth/)
- The Harvard Crimson's Early Access review (Noah F. Houghton, around Jan 2018) said "The narration is really spot-on" and "The narrator's grave tone gives the whole experience a sort of gravity it might otherwise have lacked". It also said the hex brood-adjacency rules produce "a base which ultimately looks a lot like a real ant colony". Criticisms: all content was finished in under five hours, the first mission was hard, and the $20 price depended on promised scenarios. — [The Harvard Crimson](https://thecrimson.com/article/2018/1/5/eotu-review)
- A review describes the missions as narrated by a documentary filmmaker who follows the ants and comments on the colony and the wider undergrowth. — [Gamer of Passion](https://gamerofpassion.com/empires-of-the-undergrowth-review/)
- Common player complaints in review summaries are the lack of save or pause options and hard achievements. — [GameGeeker](https://gamegeeker.com/games/empires-of-the-undergrowth); [Vaporlens summary](https://vaporlens.app/app/463530/empires_of_the_undergrowth) (aggregated summaries)
- A 2017 preview asked whether it "could be the next SimAnt". — [Indie Retro News](https://indieretronews.com/2017/11/empires-of-undergrowth-could-this-be.html)

**Mobile ant games**
- Pocket Ants: Colony Simulator is free-to-play with in-app purchases. You lead an army out of the nest to fight creatures such as tarantulas, which can be captured and added to your squad. — [Pocket Gamer](https://www.pocketgamer.com/articles/083429/pocket-ants-colony-simulator-is-a-strategy-game-for-android-that-lets-you-grow-and-command-an-ant-army/)
- Pocket Ants' store listing describes gathering resources back to the nest, upgrading nest chambers for bonuses and raiding other players. It is rated 4.3 from 555 reviews (Canadian App Store via AppFollow). One reviewer says upgrades take a long time to pool resources for or require risk. — [AppFollow](https://apps.appfollow.io/ios/pocket-ants-colony-simulator/1532712160?country=ca)
- The Ants: Underground Kingdom (StarUnion) is rated 4.5/5 from about 23K ratings (US App Store). Its store copy: "build the anthill, protect the queen, and confront crises". I found no independent critical review. — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id1568415097)

**New idle and ambient ant titles**
- *Ant* (Fruitdish; Steam page, planned release February 2027) is a "hand-drawn idle colony simulator". It starts with five ants in a glass habitat. You excavate a cabinet one section at a time, then dig on through pipes, wall cavities, soil, roots, clay, stone and underground water toward "forgotten layers far below the house". The colony can be pinned to the screen as a desktop companion while players "watch videos, browse, or play something else". It earns part of its income for up to eight hours while the player is away. — [Steam store page: Ant](https://store.steampowered.com/app/5140560/)

**Pheromone simulations (programming content)**
- Sebastian Lague published an open-source ant simulation that uses two pheromone types: one laid when leaving home, one when returning from food. — [GitHub: seblague/ant-simulation](https://github.com/seblague/ant-simulation)
- Hobbyists describe the "Ant and Slime Simulations" video's ant model: ants lay one trail while searching and another while carrying food home, and stronger paths are reinforced while weaker ones fade. The video has inspired many follow-up projects. — [Processing forum thread](https://discourse.processing.org/t/slime-and-ant-simulations/28856); [Slime Mold Simulator, itch.io](https://lordmisterman.itch.io/slime-mold-simulator)

### Inferences
- SimAnt shows that a side-view nest plus a surface view, with environmental events (rain flooding the nest, washing away trails) and outside threats (predators, humans), was enough for an ant sim to feel alive in 1991. Rain flooding the nest and erasing trails is a direct precedent for flood events in a soil cross-section.
- Empires of the Undergrowth suggests the documentary voice raises the stakes of ant behaviour for players. Its weakest point was limited content and length, which procedural variety and open-ended runs could address.
- *Ant* treats excavated soil and the layered materials found while digging as the main sense of discovery and progression. That maps directly onto a soil cross-section with hidden strata.

### Gaps
- Not researched for lack of budget: Ant Simulator (Steam), Ant Colony Simulator, "Ants!" Steam colony sims, and "fieldnotes"-style ant sim videos. I have no reception data for them.
- I could not confirm whether Empires of the Undergrowth uses fog of war or hidden soil underground. The store page and reviews retrieved don't say.
- View count of Sebastian Lague's "Coding Adventure: Ant and Slime Simulations": an aggregator in search results showed about 2.2M views and a 25 Mar 2021 upload date. I did not verify this on YouTube.
- No direct Will Wright source on why SimAnt chose a side-view nest.

## 3. Ecosystem and zero-player sims: what keeps people watching for long stretches

### Takeaway
The evidence points to a few factors. Agents should have their own needs and keep living off-screen (Rain World). There should be visible evolution or growth from simple beginnings (The Bibites, Species, Ecosystem). The player should be framed as an observer of events they set in motion (WorldBox). Most of all, there needs to be a curation layer that turns raw events into stories people retell. James Ryan's dissertation argues that raw simulation "will almost always lack story structure" and that Dwarf Fortress and The Sims succeed because players curate and retell what happens.

### Cited Findings
**Zero-player games (academic)**
- Björk and Juul's "Zero-Player Games, Or: What We Talk About When We Talk About Players" (Philosophy of Computer Games Conference, Madrid, 2012) sorts zero-player games into four types: setup-only games (input only during setup), games played by AIs, solved games, and hypothetical games. It proposes five components of being a player: human, agency, playing over time, apparent intentionality, aesthetic preferences. Conway's Game of Life is a central example. — [Juul: Zero-Player Games](https://jesperjuul.net/text/zeroplayergames/); [Juul blog, 23 May 2012](https://www.jesperjuul.net/ludologist/2012/05/23/on-zero-player-games/)
- A later critique argues that the taxonomy is too inclusive and its criteria for player input too strict. — [Aeneas Nin blog](https://aeneas-nin.bearblog.dev/on-zero-player-games/)

**Emergent narrative and curation (James Ryan, *Curating Simulated Storyworlds*, UC Santa Cruz; summarised by Emily Short, May 2019)**
- Ryan argues that emergent narrative "works more like nonfiction than fiction" because "emergent stories actually happen". His list of its appeals: "the actual", "the personal", "the uncanny and sometimes farcical", "the unauthored or co-authored", "the 'uncovered'", "the improbable", "the vast" and "the ephemeral". — [Emily Short, Ch 1–3](https://emshort.blog/2019/05/21/curating-simulated-storyworlds-james-ryan/); [dissertation, eScholarship](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/1340j5h2)
- The problems he names: "there is a ubiquitous tendency to misconstrue the raw transpiring of a simulation" as narrative, and "such material will almost always lack story structure". Boredom is the main failure, so curation is required. Short reads his claim as: what separates good emergent generators from bad ones is whether anyone is interested enough to curate them. Dwarf Fortress and The Sims succeed because people retell their stories. Short adds that interlocking systems, stakes and motivations, symbolically rich components and expressive range also matter. — [Emily Short, Ch 1–3](https://emshort.blog/2019/05/21/curating-simulated-storyworlds-james-ryan/)
- Ryan also argues that simulation elements must be small and reusable so they can recombine in interesting ways, and warns against granularity that is too coarse or too fine. — [Emily Short, Ch 4–5](https://emshort.blog/2019/05/23/curating-simulated-storyworlds-james-ryan-2/) (from search summary)

**Rain World (Videocult): creatures that live for themselves**
- Game Developer (Chris Priestman, 28 Mar 2017): Joar Jakobsson designed creatures around the question of how one could "find food and move around and get back to its home before the night comes". He says they "exist in their own right, they exist there for themselves". James Therrien: "outside of your frame they're all still alive... They're hunting food, they're getting into territorial fights." There are no fixed patrol routes, and reloading an area may place creatures differently. Downside: Jakobsson says "if you design an enemy to care for itself then, that might actually be super difficult and frustrating", and Therrien describes a streamer facing "a bunch of no-win situations" early on ("So, yes, dice rolls"). — [Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/crafting-the-complex-chaotic-ecosystem-of-i-rain-world-i-)
- Jakobsson, on The Watcher, said he was often surprised by "different ways things would lock into each other", though he could always explain the code behind them. — [Unity blog](https://unity.com/blog/exploring-procedural-design-rain-world)
- Rain World creatures are built from many individual sprites driven by real-time algorithms rather than pre-made animation sets. — [Hand and Machine class blog](https://handandmachine.org/classes/computational_fabrication/2024/08/26/introduction-logan-sullivan/)

**Dwarf Fortress**
- Tarn Adams aimed for a "fantasy world generator" in which players get a randomly generated world and earlier events shape later ones. Legends mode lets players browse the history their world contains, both generated and player-made. — [USC Game Changers exhibit](https://scalar.usc.edu/works/game-changers-2016-exhibit/dwarf-fortress)
- In a Gamescom 2026 interview with PC Gamer, Adams said a feature is worth adding when "you feel like you've played through it yourself a bunch" and you see a little narrative forming. He also contrasted emergent stories with generative AI. — [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/gaming-industry/game-development/dwarf-fortress-creator-says-we-have-a-natural-lean-towards-human-made-stories-but-but-were-all-going-to-be-very-sorely-tested-in-the-coming-years-by-ai/) (from search summary; full article not fetched)
- Adams originally conceived the project as a procedurally generated fantasy RPG. The popular Fortress mode was "just a means to an end". — [GameBanshee on a PC Gamer interview](https://www.gamebanshee.com/kdp9a)

**Evolution and ecosystem sandboxes**
- The Bibites (Léo Caussan): a "biologically-inspired 'realistic' artificial life simulation" with real-time genetic and behavioural evolution. Creatures start with an empty brain and basic genes. Over time they develop complex behaviours such as following a pheromone trail to hunt other bibites, or stockpiling food in one area of the map. A 2021 update added herding, which the developer said produced many interesting adaptations. Commenters say they found it through YouTube. A Steam version, "The Bibites: Digital Life", launched on 4 Mar 2025 at $9.99. — [The Bibites, itch.io](https://thebibites.itch.io/); [devlog 0.3.0](https://thebibites.itch.io/the-bibites/devlog/267509/the-bibites-030-artificial-life-with-herding-and-viruses); [SteamSpy](https://steamspy.com/app/2736860)
- Equilinox (ThinMatrix / Karl Wimble, solo developer; development began Oct 2015, launched Nov 2018) is a "relaxing nature simulation" where every plant and animal has its own life cycle, behaviour and needs. It was built in public through weekly YouTube devlogs. It was "Overwhelmingly Positive" (500+ reviews) at launch, and as of 13 Jul 2026 has a Player Score of 92/100 from 3,012 reviews. — [GamingOnLinux, Nov 2018](https://www.gamingonlinux.com/2018/11/relaxing-nature-sim-equilinox-is-out-seems-to-be-a-hit-with-users); [Steambase](https://steambase.io/games/equilinox/reviews); [SteamPulse](https://steampulse.org/game/853550)
- Ecosystem (Steam; Early Access 16 Mar 2021, announced on Slug Disco's site): evolving lifeforms grown from synthetic DNA in a physically simulated ocean. Creatures start with random brains and bodies, and players can steer evolution (for example, require tentacles or a certain size). Steam Workshop shares species, maps and "aquariums". Reviews are "Mostly Positive" (about 600, aggregator snapshot). — [Steam store page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1133120/Ecosystem/); [Slug Disco](https://slugdisco.com/ecosystem-swims-into-early-access-on-march-16th-2021/)
- Species: Artificial Life, Real Evolution (Quasar; Early Access Sept 2018): you can observe passively or intervene on individual creatures or whole species. It has about 74% positive of about 995 Steam reviews. One reviewer (8/10) praised being able to watch a species change over time, and criticised slow development. — [SteamPulse](https://steampulse.org/game/774541); [GamesLikeFinder review](https://gameslikefinder.com/review/species-artificial-life-real-evolution/)
- WorldBox: the "player mainly exists as an observer of the events he sets in motion". Its trailer reportedly passed 14M views in two years. Steam rating is 94% positive from about 22,300 reviews, with about 1.6M estimated players (PlayTracker estimate). — [Know Your Meme](https://knowyourmeme.com/memes/subcultures/worldbox); [PlayTracker](https://playtracker.net/insight/game/72187)

### Inferences
- The strongest design rule from these sources is that agents need their own goals and must keep simulating off-screen (Rain World). Then whatever the viewer happens to see is a slice of an ongoing life, not a staged performance. Rain World also shows the cost: when the player cannot control things, autonomy reads as unfairness. A sim the player only watches avoids most of that cost, because the player is not the one dying.
- Growth from simple beginnings (Bibites' empty brains; a colony starting from one founding queen) gives a natural long arc for watching. AntsCanada also stresses this when it says the Fire Nation "all came from just a single queen ant".
- Ryan's curation argument fits the AntsCanada evidence. The simulation should produce events, and a separate layer (chronicle, narrator, notifications, legends-style history) should pick out and present the improbable or high-stakes ones.

### Gaps
- No quantitative data on session length or idle-viewing time for any of these games.
- I did not find a confirmed GDC talk recording for Rain World's ecosystem. The Game Developer article is the main source.
- Aquarium/terrarium sims and screensaver-style sims (e.g., historical El-Fish) were not researched.
- Dwarf Fortress "Legends" quotes come from a museum exhibit text, not from Adams directly.

## 4. Procedural world variety: making each start different, and evidence on replayability

### Takeaway
The best-known caution is Kate Compton's "10,000 bowls of oatmeal": outputs that are mathematically distinct but look the same to the player add nothing. Commentators argue that variation only matters when it connects to mechanics or behaviour. Hidden underground terrain revealed by digging (Dwarf Fortress caverns, *Ant*'s layered excavation) and enemies that are not in fixed places (Rain World) are established ways to make each run different. I found no controlled study showing that procedural variety improves replayability. The evidence is designer opinion plus review sentiment.

### Cited Findings
- Kate Compton coined "procedural oatmeal". You can generate endless bowls of oatmeal, each with oats in different positions and "mathematically speaking they will all be completely unique. But the user will likely just see a lot of oatmeal." Her question is whether it matters if you have a million unique objects that players cannot perceptually tell apart. — [Wikipedia: Procedural generation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Procedural_generation); [Game Developer: Practical Procedural Generation for Everyone](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/practical-procedural-generation-for-everyone-); [MetaFilter repost of Compton's text](https://www.Metafilter.com/159678/Kate-Compton-introduces-computational-generative-methods)
- Emily Short: "the key question in oatmeal-avoidance is whether the generation is connected to anything mechanical". — [Emily Short, 21 Sep 2016](https://emshort.blog/2016/09/21/bowls-of-oatmeal-and-text-generation/)
- Boris the Brave (Jul 2026): "every bowl of oatmeal is unique, but that does not make them interesting or valuable." — [Boris the Brave, "Oatmeal Spice"](https://www.boristhebrave.com/2026/07/05/oatmeal-spice/)
- Dwarf Fortress: every subterranean tile must be revealed by digging into it. Breaking into a feature triggers a discovery message and reveals the part of the new feature in view of the dwarves. — [DF Wiki: Reveal](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/Reveal)
- Dwarf Fortress caverns are "huge natural underground tunnel systems, inhabited by strange and dangerous creatures", with three cavern layers in vanilla worlds. Players developed tactics such as carving fortifications at the edge of explored space, because creatures may lurk unseen near the edge. — [DF Wiki: Cavern](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/Cavern); [DF Wiki v0.31: Cavern](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/v0.31:Cavern)
- Rain World: no set patrol paths, and reloading an area may not place creatures in the same spots. — [Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/crafting-the-complex-chaotic-ecosystem-of-i-rain-world-i-)
- SimAnt SNES: eight scenarios, each with a different locale and hazards. This is hand-authored variety, not procedural. — [Wikipedia: SimAnt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SimAnt)
- Empires of the Undergrowth uses fixed, hand-made underground and overground maps (no procedural mode listed). Its main early-access criticism was little content, finished in under five hours. — [Steam store page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/463530/Empires_of_the_Undergrowth/); [The Harvard Crimson](https://thecrimson.com/article/2018/1/5/eotu-review)
- *Ant* (Fruitdish) arranges what you find underground as distinct strata, each a new kind of space: pipes, wall cavities, soil, roots, clay, stone, underground water. — [Steam store page: Ant](https://store.steampowered.com/app/5140560/)
- Ecosystem supports Steam Workshop sharing of species, maps and aquariums. Here player-made content provides the variety. — [Steam store page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1133120/Ecosystem/)
- Dwarf Fortress gives each player a randomly generated world with its own history, which can be browsed in Legends mode. — [USC Game Changers exhibit](https://scalar.usc.edu/works/game-changers-2016-exhibit/dwarf-fortress)

### Inferences
- For a soil cross-section, the oatmeal test suggests randomising things that change colony behaviour and the resulting story: soil strata that differ in digging cost and moisture, buried roots and stones that redirect tunnels, water tables and clay lenses that cause flooding, cavities holding other creatures, and the location of food and rivals. Purely cosmetic noise in soil texture will not register.
- Hidden terrain revealed only as ants dig gives a "discovery message" moment, as in Dwarf Fortress, that viewers can anticipate. The cross-section already gives the viewer a natural frame; fog over undug soil would preserve surprise.
- Evolution sandboxes (Bibites, Ecosystem, Species) suggest that variety in starting conditions (species traits, colony founding situation) can matter as much as terrain variety.

### Gaps
- No empirical or survey evidence linking procedural generation to measured replayability or retention in colony or ecosystem sims.
- Not researched: seed sharing in observation sims, or whether players of watch-first sims value biome variety.

## 5. UI and presentation for observation-first sims: minimal HUD, field-guide or journal styles, photo modes, documentary cameras, time-lapse

### Takeaway
The clearest evidence for presentation comes from documentary framing (Empires of the Undergrowth's narrator, praised by critics; AntsCanada's narration, praised by press), chronicle and history views (Dwarf Fortress Legends mode), and viewer participation (AntsCanada audience votes). Photo modes and in-game field journals are valued as "nature photography", but I found little ant- or ecosystem-specific evidence on HUD minimalism or time-lapse tools.

### Cited Findings
- Empires of the Undergrowth presents its wild-colony levels as a nature documentary with a narrator. In the lab levels, unseen scientists comment on experiments. A critic praised the narration ("spot-on", "grave tone" adds "gravity"). — [The Harvard Crimson](https://thecrimson.com/article/2018/1/5/eotu-review); [Steam store page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/463530/Empires_of_the_Undergrowth/)
- AntsCanada's narration turns an ordinary colony move into "a journey into Mordor", and viewers voted on the colony's new home. — [Gizmodo](https://gizmodo.com/this-narrators-infectious-intensity-makes-migrating-ant-1792158919)
- Dwarf Fortress Legends mode lets players review the world's history after the fact. — [USC Game Changers exhibit](https://scalar.usc.edu/works/game-changers-2016-exhibit/dwarf-fortress)
- James Ryan argues that a curation layer, human or machine, is needed to turn simulated events into stories. — [Emily Short on Ryan](https://emshort.blog/2019/05/21/curating-simulated-storyworlds-james-ryan/)
- The AV Club (Farouk Kannout, 20 Oct 2025) wrote: "I become a nature photographer when I play games." He described photo modes as a way to frame "such a perfect moment" and craft "digital taxidermy", and pointed to Hollow Knight: Silksong's hunter's journal as in-game creature documentation. — [The AV Club](https://www.avclub.com/how-games-turn-us-into-nature-photographers)
- Afrika (PS3 wildlife photography game): a user review praised "the most lifelike animals I've ever seen in a video game" and the manual aperture and shutter controls, but criticised a "very scripted world". GamesRadar said there was a lot of virtual nature to appreciate but little traditional action. — [Giant Bomb user review](https://www.giantbomb.com/afrika/3030-21236/user-reviews/2200-11876/); [GamesRadar](https://gamesradar.com/afrika-review)
- *Ant* (Fruitdish) offers a compact desktop companion mode, with the colony pinned to the screen while the user does other things, and a full colony view for following ants. — [Steam store page: Ant](https://store.steampowered.com/app/5140560/)
- SimAnt shipped its ant-biology education in the printed manual rather than in-game. — [Wikipedia: SimAnt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SimAnt)
- AntsCanada publishes time-lapse videos (e.g., "Time Lapse - Ants Drinking Honey"). — [Filmot](https://filmot.com/sidebyside/2caKueedluk/en/ko/English/Korean/Time+Lapse+-+Ants+Drinking+Honey)

### Inferences
- A documentary narrator or caption layer, Legends-style chronicles and named individuals are the presentation elements with the most evidence behind them. The real ant-media evidence (narration, naming, voting) and the game evidence (EotU's narrator) agree.
- Afrika's "very scripted world" criticism suggests that photo and observation tools reward unscripted behaviour. A camera or photo mode is worth more when the simulation produces unscripted moments to capture.
- A desktop companion or always-on mode (*Ant*) and time-lapse are suitable for long passive watching. Field-guide or journal styles (Silksong's hunter's journal) can deliver species and caste facts in-game, where SimAnt put them in a printed manual.

### Gaps
- No direct evidence found on minimal-HUD design in observation sims, or on documentary or cinematic camera tools in ecosystem sims (Equilinox, Bibites, Ecosystem) and how players received them.
- No data on time-lapse features in games or their effect on engagement. AntsCanada's time-lapse view counts were not retrieved.
- Not researched: Planet Zoo/Planet Coaster-style photo modes, or wildlife sims with in-game documentary cameras (e.g., WolfQuest, Beasts of Bermuda).
