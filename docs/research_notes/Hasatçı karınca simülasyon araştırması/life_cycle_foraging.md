# Messor harvester ants: colony life cycle, foraging and seed handling (M. structor first, congeners for comparison)

Source-quality legend used throughout: **[PR]** = peer-reviewed / scientific; **[HIST]** = historical primary field account (19th to mid-20th century); **[HOBBY]** = ant-keeping care sheet, vendor page or forum (captive colonies, usually uncited); **[WIKI]** = Wikipedia or similar secondary source; **[other sp.]** = number comes from a Messor species other than M. structor; **[captive]** = from lab or hobby colonies.

## Key Question 1: Founding (nuptial flight season and triggers, claustral founding, time to first workers, nanitics, pleometrosis)

### Takeaway
In M. structor sensu lato the alates are reared in late summer (from late August near Vienna), **overwinter inside the mother nest, and fly in spring**. Dealate queens are found on the ground in **April** in Central Europe. In the Mediterranean the flights are in autumn or winter (November in the Balearics). Founding is claustral, with a single queen living on her reserves and eating some of her own eggs, and the first workers appear by early summer. Some Central European populations (Austria) do not swarm at all and spread by budding as unicolonial networks. Queens can be kept together in captivity, but no peer-reviewed study shows true pleometrosis in M. structor. The "M. structor" name also hides five cryptic species, which matters for any Anatolian setting.

### Cited Findings
**Taxonomic caveat (applies to every section)**
- "Messor structor" was split into five species in 2018: M. structor s.str., M. ibericus, M. muticus, and the new species M. ponticus and M. mcarthuri. Older literature citing "M. structor" may refer to any of them. [PR] — [Steiner et al. 2018, Mol. Phylogenet. Evol. 127: 387–404](https://www.antvid.org/PDF/Steiner_et_al._-_2018_-_Molecular_Phylogenetics_and_Evolution.pdf)
- Distributions given in that revision. **M. structor s.str.:** Austria, Bulgaria, Czech Republic, France, Hungary, Romania, Slovenia. **M. ponticus:** Bulgaria, Romania, **Turkey**, Ukraine. **M. mcarthuri:** Greece, **Turkey** (holotype from Köyceğiz, Muğla). **M. muticus:** Armenia, Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, Romania, Russia, Ukraine. **M. ibericus:** Bulgaria, Croatia, France, Germany, Greece, Italy, Romania, Slovenia, Spain, Switzerland. Workers of M. ponticus and M. mcarthuri are "generally larger" than those of M. structor, M. ibericus and M. muticus. [PR] — [Steiner et al. 2018](https://www.antvid.org/PDF/Steiner_et_al._-_2018_-_Molecular_Phylogenetics_and_Evolution.pdf)
- A Russian regional treatment keeps a broad M. structor concept and disputes that the split applies to the Lower Volga. (I saw this only as a search summary and did not read the treatment itself.) — [AntCat reference page](https://antcat.org/references/143195)
- M. ibericus queens must use sperm of M. structor males to produce (hybrid) workers. They also produce M. structor males by cloning ("xenoparity"; Juvé et al., Nature 2025). As a result, colonies in parts of the range can hold two species' genomes. [PR, via secondary reports] — [Myrmecological News blog 2026](https://blog.myrmecologicalnews.org/2026/06/02/cloning-the-competition-jonathan-romiguier-on-the-bizarre-world-of-messor-harvester-ants/); [The Debrief](https://thedebrief.org/these-ant-queens-use-uncanny-cloning-abilities-to-give-birth-to-two-entirely-different-species/)
- M. barbarus also depends on hybridisation between cryptic lineages to produce workers. [PR, other sp.] — [Romiguier et al., "Cryptic lineages hybridize for worker production in the harvester ant Messor barbarus" (PMC)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5134035/)

**Nuptial flight timing and triggers**
- Near Vienna, young wingless queens that had just finished the nuptial flight were found "as early as April, but never much later". The sexual forms are present in the nests "from the end of August… where they overwinter", so the spring flight is a delayed autumn flight. In the Mediterranean (Balearic Islands) mating takes place in November (citing Eidmann). [HIST/PR] — [Molitor 1949, J. N.Y. Entomol. Soc. 57: 101–](https://archive.org/download/biostor-170978/biostor-170978.pdf)
- At Menton (Riviera), Moggridge found winged males and females in "structor" nests on 23 and 29 February, 13 March and 6 April. In M. barbarus nests he found them on 10 November, 6 December, 2 February and 10 March. These records show alates sitting in the nest through winter. [HIST] (Moggridge's "structor" at Menton may now be M. ibericus or M. structor s.str.) — [Moggridge 1873, Harvesting Ants and Trap-Door Spiders](https://gutenberg.org/cache/epub/64654/pg64654-images.html)
- Austrian "M. structor" populations at Retz did not swarm and were unicolonial, meaning nests intermingle with no clear colony boundaries. German populations, now probably M. ibericus, are reported to swarm and to be multicolonial. [PR] — [Schlick-Steiner et al. 2006, Eur. J. Entomol. 103: 469–476](https://www.eje.cz/pdfs/eje/2006/02/25.pdf); [Steiner et al. 2018](https://www.antvid.org/PDF/Steiner_et_al._-_2018_-_Molecular_Phylogenetics_and_Evolution.pdf)
- Hobbyist flight windows for M. structor vary:
  - March–September [HOBBY] — [antkeeping.info](https://antkeeping.info/ants/messor-structor)
  - March–May, peak in April, with a flight time peak "around 15:00" (activity 05:00–18:00; the site warns this may reflect when observers are out) [HOBBY] — [AntScout](https://antscout.com/species/messor-structor)
  - "spring or early summer" [HOBBY] — [Ant Shack](https://www.ant-shack.com/blogs/ant-care-sheets-1/messor-structor-the-steppe-harvester-ant)
  - May–June [HOBBY, seen via search summary] — [ameisenhaltung.de](https://www.ameisenhaltung.de/cafe/thread/6784-messor-structor/)
  - German forum view: large forms may swarm in September, while overwintered queens fly in April/May [HOBBY, via search summary] — [ameisenforum.de](https://ameisenforum.de/messor-structor-i-messor-cf-structor-ii-t31069.html)
- M. barbarus comparison: hobbyist sources put its flights in September–October (some say to November or December), often within about 48 h of the first autumn rain. Queens overwinter before any workers exist, and the first workers appear around April. [HOBBY, other sp.] — [antontop flight calendar](https://antontop.com/european-ant-nuptial-flight-calendar-2026/); [antontop M. barbarus guide](https://antontop.com/messor-barbarus-care-guide-harvester-ant/)
- I found no peer-reviewed source on the weather triggers for M. structor flights (rain, humidity, temperature thresholds).

**Claustral founding and time to first workers**
- Molitor kept two freshly dealate April queens together in a container with moist earth and fed them rice, dough and seeds. "By the beginning of the summer these larvae had developed into large and small workers." So a queen caught in April had workers by roughly June. [HIST, captive] — [Molitor 1949](https://archive.org/download/biostor-170978/biostor-170978.pdf)
- E. Meyer (1927, cited by Molitor) reported that during claustral founding M. structor queen and brood are nourished "above all at the cost of the eggs laid by the queen" and partly by other brood. One unfed queen lived **396 days** without food, eating her own brood, but did not found a colony. [HIST] — [Molitor 1949](https://archive.org/download/biostor-170978/biostor-170978.pdf)
- Hobbyist claims that the M. structor queen is fully claustral and needs no food during founding:
  - Founding tube kept "around 25°C" [HOBBY] — [formicamarket](https://formicamarket.com/nl/species/messor-structor/)
  - Same claim [HOBBY] — [AntScout](https://antscout.com/species/messor-structor)
- Hobbyist estimates of time to the first workers conflict:
  - Egg to worker 8–12 weeks [HOBBY] — [AntScout](https://antscout.com/species/messor-structor)
  - 6–10 weeks depending on temperature [HOBBY] — [Ant Shack](https://www.ant-shack.com/blogs/ant-care-sheets-1/messor-structor-the-steppe-harvester-ant)
  - 4–8 months for a solo queen (antontop, via search summary). This probably includes the winter. [HOBBY]
- M. barbarus: first workers in about 4–6 weeks at about 26 °C. [HOBBY, other sp., single source] — [antontop M. barbarus guide](https://antontop.com/messor-barbarus-care-guide-harvester-ant/)
- M. semirufus (Levant): after landing, queens search for a site and dig a burrow, and are reported to begin laying only after about 3 months. [PR abstract, other sp.] — [HUJI record, Insectes Sociaux 2017](https://cris.huji.ac.il/en/publications/forced-associations-by-young-queens-of-the-harvester-ant-messor-s/)

**Nanitics**
- First workers are smaller than mature workers. No counts are given. [HOBBY] — [AntScout](https://antscout.com/species/messor-structor)
- M. barbarus starter colonies are sold as a queen plus a few nanitics and are moved to a nest at about 30 workers. [HOBBY, other sp.] — [qualityants.nl](https://qualityants.nl/en/product/messor-barbarus-queen/)
- An M. structor keeper reported, in February of the colony's first spring, about 15 larvae and 4–10 eggs. The first new pupa was "a small minor", and about 8 pupae were counted 10 days later. [HOBBY, captive] — [ameisenforum.de keeping thread](https://ameisenforum.de/messor-structor-haltungserfahrungen-t24820-8.html)

**Pleometrosis and queen associations**
- Molitor's two M. structor foundresses "lived at complete peace with each other" before and long after the workers appeared. Eventually one was found decapitated, and the culprit (the other queen or the workers) was unknown. [HIST, captive] — [Molitor 1949](https://archive.org/download/biostor-170978/biostor-170978.pdf)
- M. semirufus: lab queen groups had no advantage in progeny number or in time to first eggs, and grouped queens died at a higher rate. Associations in the wild result from queens invading resident queens' burrows. [PR, other sp.] — [HUJI record](https://cris.huji.ac.il/en/publications/forced-associations-by-young-queens-of-the-harvester-ant-messor-s/)
- In the New World M. pergandei, queen cooperation at founding varies geographically; relatedness does not explain the variation, and aggression varies regionally. [PR, other sp., titles only] — [Insectes Sociaux 2012](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00040-012-0255-5); [Behav. Ecol. Sociobiol. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00265-013-1568-8)

### Inferences
- A plausible default for a Central/Eastern European or Anatolian M. structor-type sim:
  - Alates appear late August–September and overwinter in the nest.
  - Flights happen on warm spring days (April–May). The afternoon timing is anecdotal.
  - The queen seals herself in, lays eggs, eats some of them, and the first nanitics appear about 6–10 weeks later at 24–27 °C, so roughly June in the field.
- A Mediterranean variant would fly in autumn after the first rains and overwinter as a queen with brood.
- Optional alternative mode: in unicolonial populations (Austria), founding happens by budding rather than flights.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed data on flight weather triggers, time of day, or numbers of alates per flight for M. structor.
- No peer-reviewed count of nanitic workers or measurement of their size.
- I could not access the full text of Schlick-Steiner et al. 2005 ("Life history traits of a European Messor harvester ant", Insectes Sociaux), which probably holds the best M. structor-specific founding and sex-allocation data.

## Key Question 2: Development times, lifespans, worker polymorphism and what majors do

### Takeaway
No peer-reviewed brood-development times exist for M. structor. Hobbyist figures cluster at **about 8–12 weeks egg to adult at 22–27 °C**, with the eggs-to-hatch stage notably long. Messor workers show **continuous (monophasic) polymorphism**: in M. barbarus worker mass spans about 1.3–39 mg, and M. structor workers measure about 3–10 mm. Majors forage little and are thought to mill and crack seeds inside the nest. Worker lifespan of 1–2 years and queen lifespan of 15–25 years are hobbyist estimates only.

### Cited Findings
- M. structor stage durations: eggs hatch in **24–29 days**, larvae pupate after **16–23 days**, pupae emerge after **17–28 days**, for a total of **8–12 weeks**, and development is temperature-dependent. Keeping temperature is 22–26 °C. [HOBBY; the page attributes these numbers to "Metzl et al. 2018", which I could not verify] — [AntScout](https://antscout.com/species/messor-structor)
- 6–10 weeks total development, with a warm zone of 25–28 °C and brood-chamber humidity of 50–60%. [HOBBY] — [Ant Shack](https://www.ant-shack.com/blogs/ant-care-sheets-1/messor-structor-the-steppe-harvester-ant)
- The ameisenhaltung.de profile lists the M. structor stage times as "k.A." (unknown). [HOBBY, via search summary] — [ameisenhaltung.de](https://www.ameisenhaltung.de/cafe/thread/6784-messor-structor/)
- M. barbarus: about 10–14 days from egg to larva, and about 3–5 weeks from egg to worker, strongly temperature-dependent. [HOBBY, other sp., via search summary; this looks too fast compared with other figures] — [myants.de M. barbarus](https://www.myants.de/messor-barbarus/)
- **Worker size, M. structor**, by source:
  - 4.0–9.5 mm (queen 9.5–10.5 mm, male 7–8 mm) [HOBBY] — [antkeeping.info](https://antkeeping.info/ants/messor-structor)
  - 4.5–9 mm (queen 11–14 mm) [HOBBY] — [formicamarket](https://formicamarket.com/nl/species/messor-structor/)
  - Minors about 4–6 mm, majors "up to 10–12 mm", queens about 12–15 mm [HOBBY] — [Ant Shack](https://www.ant-shack.com/blogs/ant-care-sheets-1/messor-structor-the-steppe-harvester-ant)
  - 3–9 mm, with minors as small as 2.2 mm and majors 8–9 mm (page cites Casevitz-Weulersse 1990 and Kutter 1977) [HOBBY] — [AntScout](https://antscout.com/species/messor-structor)
  - Roughly 4–10 mm [WIKI] — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Messor_structor)
- **Polymorphism type, M. barbarus [PR, other sp.]** — [Bernadou et al. 2016, J. Exp. Biol. 219: 2920](https://journals.biologists.com/jeb/article/219/18/2920/15446/Ergonomics-of-load-transport-in-the-seed)
  - The worker caste shows "continuous monophasic allometry" between head mass and thorax length (allometric coefficient 1.363, R² = 0.974).
  - Fresh mass ranged 1.65–37.56 mg in a morphometric sample of 57 workers and 1.30–38.90 mg in field-tested ants.
  - Size classes were defined as small (<5 mg, mean 3.42 mg), medium (5–10 mg, mean 6.95 mg) and large (>10 mg, mean 16.98 mg).
  - Mandible gap is 0.80–2.80 mm.
- M. capensis also shows monophasic allometry. M. barbarus, M. capitatus and M. pergandei are described as highly polymorphic. [PR, other sp.] — [Plowes et al. 2013, Myrmecol. News 18: 33–49](https://myrmecologicalnews.org/cms/index.php?option=com_download&view=download&filename=volume18%2Fmn18_33-49_printable.pdf&format=raw)
- Molitor (1949) described Messor workers as "typically dimorphic". This is an older view; modern morphometrics show a continuum. [HIST] — [Molitor 1949](https://archive.org/download/biostor-170978/biostor-170978.pdf)
- **Majors' roles:**
  - M. barbarus large workers are "most often used for tasks other than transporting food". The authors suggest they mill seeds inside the nest and note majors' "weak participation in foraging columns". [PR, other sp.] — [Bernadou et al. 2016](https://journals.biologists.com/jeb/article/219/18/2920/15446/Ergonomics-of-load-transport-in-the-seed)
  - In M. barbarus, minors carry small seeds such as oat fragments most efficiently, majors harvest larger or preferred seeds, and media workers do most trail-laying. [WIKI, other sp.] — [Wikipedia M. barbarus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Messor_barbarus)
  - At Menton, it was "one of the largest workers" that grabbed and carried off a porcelain bead dropped on a trail. [HIST] — [Moggridge 1873](https://gutenberg.org/cache/epub/64654/pg64654-images.html)
  - Hobby sources say M. structor majors have large, square heads with strong muscles for crushing seeds [HOBBY] — [Ant Shack](https://www.ant-shack.com/blogs/ant-care-sheets-1/messor-structor-the-steppe-harvester-ant)
  - Majors grind the seeds and minors work the pieces into "flour" with saliva [HOBBY] — [AntScout](https://antscout.com/species/messor-structor)
  - Majors appear only "in the second or third year" [HOBBY] — [formicamarket](https://formicamarket.com/nl/species/messor-structor/)
- **Heat tolerance by size:** in M. ebeninus, workers' tolerance of high temperatures *decreased* with body size. M. ebeninus and M. arenarius did not differ in CTmin or CTmax. [PR, other sp.] — [O'Donnell et al. 2020, Insectes Sociaux 67: 473–479](https://works.swarthmore.edu/fac-biology/729)
- **Worker lifespan:** M. barbarus workers "typically live 1–2 years". [HOBBY, other sp., via search summary] — [Ant Shack M. barbarus](https://ant-shack.com/blogs/ant-care-sheets-1/messor-barbarus-european-harvester-ant-care-sheet)
- **Queen lifespan:**
  - M. barbarus 15–20 years [HOBBY, via search summary] — [Ant Shack M. barbarus](https://ant-shack.com/blogs/ant-care-sheets-1/messor-barbarus-european-harvester-ant-care-sheet)
  - M. wasmanni and M. cephalotes about 20–25 years [HOBBY, via search summary] — [antsite.eu M. wasmanni](https://antsite.eu/en/ants/messor-wasmanni/)
  - M. aciculatus up to 25 years [HOBBY, via search summary] — [antantics.wales](https://www.antantics.wales/product-page/messor-aciculatus-harvester-ant)

### Inferences
- Sim defaults (all hobbyist-grade):
  - At 24–27 °C, use roughly 3–4 weeks as egg, 2–3 weeks as larva and 2.5–4 weeks as pupa (about 60–80 days total). Development should slow sharply below about 20 °C and stop in winter.
  - Model worker size as a continuous variable, e.g. 1.3–39 mg mass, or 3–10 mm head-to-gaster length with head width scaling more steeply than body.
  - Large workers mostly stay inside to crack and mill seeds. Small and medium workers do most foraging and trail-laying.
  - Majors start appearing in year 2–3.
  - Workers live about 1–2 years and queens 15–25 years. Treat both as estimates.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed brood-development tables for any Messor species were found. The "Metzl et al. 2018" attribution on AntScout could not be located.
- No peer-reviewed worker longevity data (minors vs majors) or field queen longevity data for any Messor species.
- No M. structor head-width ranges in mm from a primary source were found. Steiner et al. 2018 has morphometric discriminant functions, but I did not extract absolute head widths.

## Key Question 3: Colony growth (year 1–3 numbers, mature size, onset of alate production, monogyny vs polygyny)

### Takeaway
Growth is slow. Captive data suggest a colony has **about 100 workers after 1–2 years**: two-year-old lab M. barbarus colonies averaged about 120 workers. Mature M. structor colonies are usually reported at **hundreds to about 5,000 workers**, with claims up to 10,000 or "tens of thousands" in hobby sources. M. structor s.l. is listed as polygynous, and some Austrian populations are unicolonial, spreading by nest budding. M. barbarus is treated as monogynous by keepers. Alate production timing (late summer) is known, but quantities are not.

### Cited Findings
- **Captive growth to about 100 workers:**
  - "Expect 1–2 years to reach 100+ workers", with a nest upgrade at 30–50 workers. [HOBBY] — [AntScout](https://antscout.com/species/messor-structor)
  - M. structor colonies are "not the fastest brood-rearing species" and majors appear in year 2–3. [HOBBY] — [formicamarket](https://formicamarket.com/nl/species/messor-structor/)
- **M. barbarus, captive:** four 2-year-old colonies raised in captivity from founding queens averaged **about 120 workers**, and their granaries were almost full at about 8 g of seeds. [PR, other sp., captive; seen in a search abstract snippet] — [Reyes-López & Fernández-Haeger 2002, "Food storage in the nest and seed selectivity in the harvester ant Messor barbarus"](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/232717100_Food_storage_in_the_nest_and_seed_selectivity_in_the_harvester_ant_Messor_barbarus_Hymenoptera_Formicidae)
- **Mature size, M. structor**, by source:
  - "Up to 1–2 thousand workers" (page cites Radchenko & Dudka 2002, Kaniv Reserve, Ukraine) [HOBBY citing PR] — [AntScout](https://antscout.com/species/messor-structor)
  - Up to about 5,000 in the wild, marked "citation needed" [WIKI] — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Messor_structor)
  - Russian keeper pages: usually not more than 5,000, "more often several hundred" [HOBBY] — [antclub.org](http://antclub.org/Myrmicinae/Messor/Messor_structor); [mr-ant.ru](https://mr-ant.ru/blog/muravi-messor-structor/)
  - Maximum 10,000 [HOBBY] — [formicamarket](https://formicamarket.com/nl/species/messor-structor/)
  - "Tens of thousands" [HOBBY] — [Ant Shack](https://www.ant-shack.com/blogs/ant-care-sheets-1/messor-structor-the-steppe-harvester-ant)
- **Comparison scale (New World, other sp.):** individually foraging Messor colonies have about 500 workers, group foragers several thousand, and M. pergandei foraging trails contain 17,000–35,000 individuals. [PR, other sp.] — [Plowes et al. 2013](https://myrmecologicalnews.org/cms/index.php?option=com_download&view=download&filename=volume18%2Fmn18_33-49_printable.pdf&format=raw)
- **Queen number and colony structure:**
  - M. structor is listed as polygynous [HOBBY] — [antkeeping.info](https://antkeeping.info/ants/messor-structor)
  - Some populations are unicolonial, lack swarming flights, and new colonies "may form through fission" [HOBBY citing PR] — [AntScout](https://antscout.com/species/messor-structor)
  - Austrian populations (Retz) are unicolonial with no swarming; German ones are multicolonial and swarm [PR] — [Schlick-Steiner et al. 2006](https://www.eje.cz/pdfs/eje/2006/02/25.pdf)
  - In many areas nests form unicolonial supercolonies, and there was no clear aggression between neighbouring nests in Austria [WIKI citing Schlick-Steiner 2006] — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Messor_structor)
  - M. barbarus is listed as monogyne [HOBBY, other sp.] — [qualityants.nl](https://qualityants.nl/en/product/messor-barbarus-queen/)
- **Alate production:**
  - Sexuals are present in M. structor nests near Vienna from the end of August and overwinter there. [HIST] — [Molitor 1949](https://archive.org/download/biostor-170978/biostor-170978.pdf)
  - Alates were present at Menton from February to April (structor) and November to March (barbarus). [HIST] — [Moggridge 1873](https://gutenberg.org/cache/epub/64654/pg64654-images.html)
  - In M. pergandei, supplemental food and removal of neighbours increased alates produced per worker, and food added while reproductive brood was being laid increased female alate production. [PR, other sp.] — [Plowes et al. 2013, citing Ryti & Case 1988; Ode & Rissing 2002](https://myrmecologicalnews.org/cms/index.php?option=com_download&view=download&filename=volume18%2Fmn18_33-49_printable.pdf&format=raw)

### Inferences
- Suggested growth curve for a sim (low confidence, mostly hobbyist), assuming one founding queen in spring:
  - End of year 1: about 10–40 workers.
  - End of year 2: about 100–200.
  - Year 3: several hundred, with majors appearing.
  - Year 4–6: maturity at about 1,000–5,000 workers. Allow rare large colonies of up to 10,000.
- First alates should appear only once the colony is mature, probably year 3–5, which is an inference. They are reared in late summer and overwinter in the nest.
- Food availability should raise alate output, as the M. pergandei data show.
- Polygyny and budding are optional "unicolonial population" modes.

### Gaps
- No field census of M. structor worker numbers by colony age was found.
- No data were found on the age at first alate production or on the number of alates per colony for any European Messor.
- The Radchenko & Dudka (2002) Ukrainian source was seen only through an aggregator citation.

## Key Question 4: Seasonality and daily activity (Central/Eastern Europe, Anatolia, winter dormancy, summer heat pauses, temperature windows, night foraging)

### Takeaway
Messor activity is gated by soil and surface temperature and by humidity. In M. barbarus (Spain), surface activity starts above critical internal soil temperature and humidity values, and worker departures peak at **about 25–30 °C surface temperature**. Foraging is high roughly between **15 and 35 °C** at the surface. In hot summers activity becomes bimodal (early morning and late afternoon), and some sources say M. structor becomes mostly nocturnal in summer. Mediterranean colonies harvest in spring and autumn, with almost no seed collection from mid-January to mid-March. Captive Central European M. structor colonies are given about 2–4 months of dormancy at about 8–15 °C (November to February or March).

### Cited Findings
- **M. barbarus surface activity** [PR, other sp.] — [Azcárate, Kovacs & Peco 2007, J. Insect Behav. 20: 315–329](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10905-007-9074-3), summary via search results
  - Study: 44 colonies in Mediterranean grassland near Madrid, followed March 1998 to September 1999.
  - Colonies became active above critical values of internal soil temperature and relative humidity.
  - Surface temperature was the main regulator of worker departure rate, which peaked at about 25–30 °C.
  - Walking speed rose with temperature, less so in large workers.
- A later review states that M. barbarus foraging "is high as long as the surface temperatures are between approximately 15 and 35 °C" (citing Azcárate et al. 2007 and Westerman et al. 2012). [PR secondary, other sp., via search summary] — [Azcárate et al. 2007 record](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10905-007-9074-3)
- **M. barbarus in summer (Barcelona) [PR, other sp.]** — [Bernadou et al. 2016](https://journals.biologists.com/jeb/article/219/18/2920/15446/Ergonomics-of-load-transport-in-the-seed)
  - Fieldwork was done during the "early-morning and late-afternoon activity peaks".
  - Ground temperatures during trials ranged 17.9–48.1 °C.
  - Travel time fell with temperature: log10(travel time) = 2.512 − 0.015 × T.
- M. barbarus in summer (mid-June to mid-August, Mediterranean forest): as temperatures rise through the morning, foragers progressively shorten the foraging column. [PR, other sp., abstract only] — [Environ. ### Inferences
- Suggested sim rules:
  - Foraging starts when surface temperature is above about 15 °C and the nest soil is warm and moist enough. Outflow peaks at 25–30 °C and stops above about 35 °C surface temperature.
  - In midsummer this produces morning and evening (or nocturnal) peaks with a midday pause.
  - Rain, strong wind and cold close the nest.
  - Steppe/Anatolian year: dormancy from about November to February/March with the colony clustered deep and brood mostly larvae (an inference). Spring harvest runs April–June. A heat-limited July–August shifts to crepuscular or nocturnal activity, followed by an autumn harvest in September–October. Alates are reared in late summer and overwinter.
  - Large workers should cope with heat better in water-loss terms, but M. ebeninus data show large workers have *lower* heat tolerance, so any size-based heat rule is uncertain.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed field study of M. structor seasonal or daily activity in Central/Eastern Europe, Ukraine or Anatolia was retrievable. The Radchenko & Dudka 2002 Ukrainian data was seen only second-hand.
- No measured hibernation soil temperatures from wild M. structor nests.
- I did not get the exact critical soil temperature and humidity thresholds from Azcárate et al. 2007, only the abstract-level description.

## Key Question 5: Foraging (trails vs individual, trail length, foragers, seed choice and size, seasonal seed sources, insects/carrion, recruitment and pheromones, weather)

### Takeaway
M. structor is mainly an **individual forager** with strong site fidelity. It **recruits nestmates to dense seed patches** using Dufour gland trail pheromone plus stridulation, and uses poison-gland secretion for alarm. Its trails are **broad (≥1 m), diffuse and low-density**. Congeners span the full range:
- M. capitatus and M. aciculatus forage individually.
- M. bouvieri uses rotating columns 1.5–26 m long.
- M. barbarus uses permanent trunk trails up to 30 m that persist over winter.
- M. ebeninus forms long trails.

Seeds are taken from many plant families, including whole green capsules cut from plants. Dead insects and carcasses are taken opportunistically.

### Cited Findings
- **M. structor foraging mode [PR]** — [Plowes et al. 2013, Tab. 1 and text](https://myrmecologicalnews.org/cms/index.php?option=com_download&view=download&filename=volume18%2Fmn18_33-49_printable.pdf&format=raw)
  - "Foraging columns or trunk trails (unclear), irregular, broad trails (1 m wide) (Baroni Urbani 1991), individual foraging on widespread seeds, dense seeds exploited through recruitment (Hahn & Maschwitz 1985)."
  - "Broad trails (more than 1 m wide) with low densities of foragers… workers drop seeds which are later recovered, but not at specific seed depots."
  - "Both M. aciculatus and M. structor foragers show strong site fidelity, where individuals return on subsequent foraging trips to areas they have just searched."
- Recruitment intensity in M. structor increases with seed availability (Hahn & Maschwitz 1985). [PR, secondary] — [Plowes et al. 2013](https://myrmecologicalnews.org/cms/index.php?option=com_download&view=download&filename=volume18%2Fmn18_33-49_printable.pdf&format=raw)
- Hobbyist claim of long foraging columns of 10–20 m in M. structor. [HOBBY, conflicts with the "individual forager" description] — [AntScout](https://antscout.com/species/messor-structor)
- **Pheromones [PR]** — [Plowes et al. 2013, Tab. 2](https://myrmecologicalnews.org/cms/index.php?option=com_download&view=download&filename=volume18%2Fmn18_33-49_printable.pdf&format=raw)
  - M. structor uses the Dufour gland for recruitment and the poison gland for alarm (Hahn & Maschwitz 1985).
  - In M. capitatus, M. minor, M. structor and M. wasmanni, recruitment involves Dufour gland secretions plus stridulation.
  - In M. capitatus, stridulation speeds recruitment but does not increase the number recruited. Dufour trail extract persists about 30 min after reinforcement stops and is not colony-specific.
  - M. barbarus uses the Dufour gland for recruitment (stronger than poison-gland extract on 10 cm-radius test trails) and the poison gland for alarm and defence. Recruitment is augmented by stridulation.
  - M. ebeninus uses the Dufour gland for recruitment.
- **Congeners' foraging strategies [PR, other sp.]** — [Plowes et al. 2013](https://myrmecologicalnews.org/cms/index.php?option=com_download&view=download&filename=volume18%2Fmn18_33-49_printable.pdf&format=raw)
  - **M. barbarus:**
    - Trunk trails are a stable system of permanent trails that persist over winter even when unused.
    - Trails are 1–30 m long and about 10–20 cm wide, often branched.
    - Productive seasons give more branching and shorter links; poor seasons give longer links.
    - Seed depots occur on about 20% of paths.
    - Colonies have multiple nest entrances, and a third of colonies in one Spanish population had trails linking their own entrances.
    - Size-matching between ants and loads declines with distance from the nest.
  - **M. bouvieri:** foraging columns on temporary seasonal trails 1.5–26 m long (4–5 m in one study) that rotate around the nest, sometimes within a day. 21–32% of variation in item size is explained by ant size.
  - **M. capitatus:**
    - Individual foraging about 90% of the time, with some group foraging and trunk trails.
    - Narrow trails under 10 cm wide with seed depots.
    - Trail direction is shaped by neighbours, and colony-specific "anal spots" mark the area around the nest.
  - **M. aciculatus:** individual foraging, with ritualized one-on-one duels between workers of neighbouring colonies, without recruiting help.
  - **M. ebeninus:** "long foraging trails", and may produce more than one column per day. It is categorised variously as individual or group forager, and seasonally changes seed types and patch use.
- **Seed size and choice [PR, other sp.]** — [Plowes et al. 2013](https://myrmecologicalnews.org/cms/index.php?option=com_download&view=download&filename=volume18%2Fmn18_33-49_printable.pdf&format=raw); [Wikipedia M. barbarus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Messor_barbarus)
  - M. barbarus prefers seeds of **4–50 mg** regardless of seed density, and also selects by seed length.
  - Seeds under 0.4 mg are rarely selected [WIKI].
  - Given the same food (oats) in different sizes, workers chose smaller items.
  - Selectivity is positively correlated with trail length.
  - In M. capitatus, when "seed" size was held constant at 5–5.5 mm diameter, artificial seeds of about 400 mg were preferred. One ant carrying a wheat grain over 20 m uses less than 1 J.
- **Load handling, M. barbarus [PR, other sp.]** — [Bernadou et al. 2016](https://journals.biologists.com/jeb/article/219/18/2920/15446/Ergonomics-of-load-transport-in-the-seed)
  - Small ants were offered items averaging 9.47 times their mass but collected items averaging 5.41 times their mass.
  - Dragged items averaged about 3.5 times the mass of carried items.
- **Seed sources (Mediterranean, structor and barbarus at Menton) [HIST]** — [Moggridge 1873](https://gutenberg.org/cache/epub/64654/pg64654-images.html)
  - Taxa recorded: fumitory (*Fumaria*, three species), *Alyssum maritimum*, *Oxalis corniculata*, *Silene*, *Linum gallicum*, mallow (*Lavatera*), medick (*Medicago*), wild lentil (*Ervum*), spiny broom (*Cytisus*), *Valerianella*, *Centaurea*, grasses (*Tragus racemosus* filled one granary; canary grass and oats were taken), pellitory, *Polygonum aviculare*, *Cynoglossum* nutlets, hemp and peas.
  - One nest held seeds from more than 12 species in at least 7 families.
  - Ants cut green shepherd's-purse (*Capsella bursa-pastoris*) pods by twisting the stalk until it snapped, then dragged them home.
  - Foragers travelled to weedy cultivated terraces rather than the nearby scrub. One continuous double column was **24 yards (about 22 m)** long, and one nest was about 10 yards (about 9 m) from its forage.
- M. structor removes seed appendages (e.g., the caruncle of *Mercurialis annua*) before storage, which is said to inhibit germination. [WIKI citing Pacini 1990, Acta Bot. Neerl. 39: 253–262] — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Messor_structor)
- A major M. barbarus worker was photographed carrying a *Platanus hybrida* (plane tree) seed in Seville. [PR, figure caption] — [Plowes et al. 2013](https://myrmecologicalnews.org/cms/index.php?option=com_download&view=download&filename=volume18%2Fmn18_33-49_printable.pdf&format=raw)
- **Animal food [HIST]** — [Moggridge 1873](https://gutenberg.org/cache/epub/64654/pg64654-images.html)
  - Structor and barbara "at least in times of scarcity, eagerly devour animal food". A few ants in harvesting trains carry small dead insects or crushed land-snail shells.
  - "Large numbers of structors" were seen "picking the bones of a dead lizard".
  - M. barbarus attacked a live caterpillar and dismembered a dead grasshopper.
  - No aphid tending was ever seen in structor or barbara.
- In captivity M. structor accepts insects (crickets, fruit flies, mealworm pieces) "once or twice a week", especially while rearing brood. [HOBBY] — [formicamarket](https://formicamarket.com/nl/species/messor-structor/); [Ant Shack](https://www.ant-shack.com/blogs/ant-care-sheets-1/messor-structor-the-steppe-harvester-ant)
- **Colony competition and robbing:**
  - M. barbarus colonies raid the seed stores of conspecific neighbours. Moggridge saw a robbing train between nests 15 feet (about 4.6 m) apart, with fights along it. [HIST] — [Moggridge 1873](https://gutenberg.org/cache/epub/64654/pg64654-images.html)
  - M. pergandei columns of neighbouring colonies avoid each other, and intersections cause fights. [PR, other sp.] — [Plowes et al. 2013](https://myrmecologicalnews.org/cms/index.php?option=com_download&view=download&filename=volume18%2Fmn18_33-49_printable.pdf&format=raw)
- **Navigation:** M. barbarus uses celestial (sun) cues to return to the nest (Santschi 1911). Individually foraging M. semirufus oriented correctly under blue sky but not under overcast. [PR, other sp.] — [Plowes et al. 2013](https://myrmecologicalnews.org/cms/index.php?option=com_download&view=download&filename=volume18%2Fmn18_33-49_printable.pdf&format=raw)
- **Crop-field context (NE Spain) [PR, other sp.]** — [Baraibar et al. 2011, recercat record](https://recercat.cat/handle/10459.1/58631); [Wikipedia M. barbarus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Messor_barbarus)
  - M. barbarus removes large quantities of weed seeds in spring and summer.
  - It is reported to cause 50–100% of seed losses in arable fields.
  - In winter cereals, yield loss was 0.2% at sowing and 0.6% at harvest, with a maximum of 9.2%.

### Inferences
- For M. structor, model scouts searching individually in a wide fan.
- When a scout finds a dense patch, it lays a Dufour-gland trail on the way home and stridulates. The trail decays over about 30 min unless reinforced, giving a temporary, broad (≥1 m), low-density "highway" rather than a narrow cleared trunk trail.
- Foragers return to their last successful sector (site fidelity).
- Optional species presets:
  - "M. barbarus": permanent cleared trails of 1–30 m, 10–20 cm wide.
  - "M. bouvieri": columns of 1.5–26 m that rotate daily.
- Seed menu weighting: grasses plus small-seeded forbs (Brassicaceae, Fumariaceae, Caryophyllaceae, Fabaceae, Asteraceae, Polygonaceae), with green pods cut from plants. Prey or carrion is a minor item that rises when seeds are scarce.

### Gaps
- No numeric data on M. structor trail length, forager counts per trail, or foraging range.
- No seed species lists for Pannonian, Ukrainian or Anatolian M. structor-group populations were retrieved.
- No quantitative data on wind effects. Rain stopping foraging is documented only qualitatively (Moggridge).

## Key Question 6: Seed processing and storage (granaries, husking, middens, milling into "ant bread", feeding larvae, germination control, sun-drying, mould)

### Takeaway
Seeds are carried into **horizontal granary chambers** about 2.5–51 cm below the surface, each roughly pocket-watch sized. They are husked inside, and chaff, empty capsules and seed coats are dumped on a **refuse mound (midden)** at the entrance, where dropped seeds sprout. Stored seeds stay moist but rarely germinate. When they do, workers **bite off the radicle and carry the seeds out to dry in the sun**, then bring them back, and the ants preferentially eat these "malted" seeds. Inside, seeds are crushed and chewed into a moist dough ("ant bread", a hobbyist term) that is fed to larvae. Majors are thought to do much of the milling.

### Cited Findings
- **Granaries [HIST]** — [Moggridge 1873](https://gutenberg.org/cache/epub/64654/pg64654-images.html)
  - In M. barbarus, granaries "lay from an inch and a half to six inches below the surface (about 3.8–15 cm) and were all horizontal… the average granary being about as large as a gentleman's gold watch".
  - "The granaries of A. structor are arranged in the same way… lying at depths below the surface, varying from one to twenty inches (about 2.5–51 cm)."
  - Where soil is shallow, granaries are crowded together, and some barbarus nests were cut into sandstone rock.
- **Granary contents:**
  - A **2-year-old captive M. barbarus colony of about 120 workers held about 8 g of seeds** in near-full granaries. The mass of stored seeds relates to the number of workers and larvae (citing Gentry 1974). [PR, other sp., captive] — [Reyes-López & Fernández-Haeger 2002](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/232717100_Food_storage_in_the_nest_and_seed_selectivity_in_the_harvester_ant_Messor_barbarus_Hymenoptera_Formicidae)
  - Claim that M. structor stores "1–2 kg or more", marked "citation needed". [WIKI, unverified] — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Messor_structor)
- **Nest form, M. structor:** no above-ground structure, only a small earth mound with discarded husks around it. Underground it is a long vertical tunnel with side galleries and chambers, used for several years. [HOBBY, Russian-language pages, low confidence] — [antclub.org](http://antclub.org/Myrmicinae/Messor/Messor_structor); [mr-ant.ru](https://mr-ant.ru/blog/muravi-messor-structor/)
- **Husking and middens [HIST]** — [Moggridge 1873](https://gutenberg.org/cache/epub/64654/pg64654-images.html)
  - While some workers bring in supplies, "others are busy sorting the materials thus obtained, stripping off all the useless envelopes of seed or grain, and carrying them out to throw away".
  - The "kitchen middens" consist "principally of plant-refuse such as the chaff of grasses, empty capsules, gnawed seed-coats" mixed with excavated earth, and "speedily rise in the harvest season".
  - Husks of seeds taken in one day appear on the rubbish heap the next day.
  - Patches of seedlings 1–3 feet (about 0.3–0.9 m) across grow on and around the refuse.
- **Germination control and sun-drying [HIST]** — [Moggridge 1873](https://gutenberg.org/cache/epub/64654/pg64654-images.html)
  - Of "thousands of seeds" from 21 nests (late October to May), only 27 seeds in 7 nests showed germination, and 11 of these had been mutilated to arrest growth. Sprouted seeds were found only from November to February.
  - Granary seeds removed from the nest germinated readily, with seedlings up within about 3 weeks.
  - "After the radicle… has been gnawed off, they are brought out from the nest and placed in the sun to dry, and then, after a sufficient exposure, carried below into the nest. The seeds are thus in effect malted… I have myself witnessed the avidity with which the contents of seeds thus treated are devoured."
  - "Sometimes, after a shower of rain, they will be brought out by the ants with the point of the little root… gnawed off."
  - In his experiments, captive ants liked malted seeds best.
- Moggridge's 1874 letter to Darwin and an 1875 Nature review report his experiments: the ants' presence was needed to prevent germination, but presence alone did not stop it. [HIST] — [Darwin Correspondence Project](https://www.darwinproject.ac.uk/letter/DCP-LETT-9339.xml); [Nature 1875](https://nature.com/articles/011245a0.pdf)
- A modern review contrasts Messor barbarus, which destroys the radicle of intact seeds, with *Pheidole* and *Solenopsis* species, which dry damp seeds outside the nest after rain. In *Pogonomyrmex badius*, germinating seeds are fed to larvae. [PR, other genera] — [Tschinkel & Kwapich 2016 (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5125654/)
- Russian keeper pages describe the same behaviour: grain is kept in damp chambers and periodically sprouts, and workers bite off the sprouts, grind the seeds to powder, moisten it with saliva and feed it to larvae. [HOBBY, low confidence] — [antclub.org](http://antclub.org/Myrmicinae/Messor/Messor_structor)
- **Larval feeding and "ant bread":**
  - An M. structor keeper reports a growing food clump ("Ameisenbrot"). Grains that sprouted in the humid chamber were treated as waste rather than eaten. [HOBBY, captive] — [ameisenforum.de](https://ameisenforum.de/messor-structor-haltungserfahrungen-t24820-8.html)
  - Seeds are husked and chewed into a paste ("bread") with saliva and water. [HOBBY] — [Ant Shack](https://www.ant-shack.com/blogs/ant-care-sheets-1/messor-structor-the-steppe-harvester-ant)
  - Majors grind and minors make "flour" with amylase-rich saliva. [HOBBY] — [AntScout](https://antscout.com/species/messor-structor)
  - "Ant bread" is a keeper term. I found no peer-reviewed description of the larval feeding sequence in M. structor.
- **Milling by majors:** in M. barbarus, majors probably mill seeds inside the nest and take little part in foraging columns. [PR, other sp.] — [Bernadou et al. 2016](https://journals.biologists.com/jeb/article/219/18/2920/15446/Ergonomics-of-load-transport-in-the-seed)
- **Nest commensals and mould:** granaries of structor and barbara commonly contain springtails, silverfish (*Lepisma*), small white woodlice and occasionally click-beetle (elater) larvae. Moggridge noted that sand "does not mold". No Messor-specific mould-control mechanism is described in these sources. [HIST] — [Moggridge 1873](https://gutenberg.org/cache/epub/64654/pg64654-images.html); [Molitor 1949](https://archive.org/download/biostor-170978/biostor-170978.pdf)
- **Captive humidity behaviour:** M. structor colonies choose dry chambers and need only one small humidified area, with nest humidity of 30–60%. [HOBBY] — [formicamarket](https://formicamarket.com/nl/species/messor-structor/); [ameisenforum.de](https://ameisenforum.de/messor-structor-haltungserfahrungen-t24820-8.html)

### Inferences
- Sim pipeline:
  1. Forager drops the seed at the entrance or a granary.
  2. Sorters husk it (chaff goes to the midden).
  3. Clean seeds are stacked in shallow horizontal granaries, a few to 50 cm deep.
  4. A miller (major) cracks the seed.
  5. Nurses chew it into dough and feed larvae. Adults also feed on seed contents.
- After rain or in damp granaries, a fraction of seeds sprout. Workers bite off the radicle and, on the next sunny day, haul the seeds to the surface to dry, then return them. This makes a strongly visible behaviour.
- The midden grows during harvest seasons and sprouts seedlings.
- Granary mass should scale with workers plus larvae. Using about 8 g per 120 workers as a captive anchor gives about 0.07 g of stored seed per worker (an inference).

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed measurement of granary number, volume or seed mass in wild M. structor nests. The "1–2 kg" figure is unsourced.
- No peer-reviewed study of mould or fungal control (e.g., antimicrobial secretions) in Messor granaries was found.
- The roles of minors vs majors in husking and milling are not quantified for M. structor.

## Key Question 7: Daily seed intake per worker or per colony

### Takeaway
I found **no direct measurement** of daily seed intake per worker or per colony for M. structor or any European Messor. The usable proxies are captive granary mass (about 8 g for about 120 workers after 2 years in M. barbarus), foraging-trip counts from a New World Messor (about 2,700–3,900 trips per colony per day), and seed-mass preferences (4–50 mg in M. barbarus).

### Cited Findings
- M. andrei (California, New World, monomorphic): total colony foraging trips were estimated at **2,684–3,933 per day** (Brown 1997). Most foraging is within 5 m of the nest, with columns up to 20 m. [PR, other sp.] — [Plowes et al. 2013](https://myrmecologicalnews.org/cms/index.php?option=com_download&view=download&filename=volume18%2Fmn18_33-49_printable.pdf&format=raw)
- Captive M. barbarus colonies of about 120 workers (2 years old) had near-full granaries of about 8 g. [PR, other sp., captive] — [Reyes-López & Fernández-Haeger 2002](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/232717100_Food_storage_in_the_nest_and_seed_selectivity_in_the_harvester_ant_Messor_barbarus_Hymenoptera_Formicidae)
- M. barbarus preferred seed mass is 4–50 mg. [PR, other sp.] — [Plowes et al. 2013](https://myrmecologicalnews.org/cms/index.php?option=com_download&view=download&filename=volume18%2Fmn18_33-49_printable.pdf&format=raw)
- In one M. pergandei experiment, seeds were added to colonies in amounts equal to a colony's average monthly calorific intake (Gordon 1978), but the value is not given in the review. [PR, other sp.] — [Plowes et al. 2013](https://myrmecologicalnews.org/cms/index.php?option=com_download&view=download&filename=volume18%2Fmn18_33-49_printable.pdf&format=raw)
- Density-dependent removal in M. barbarus was tested with *Lolium multiflorum* seeds at 1,000–20,000 seeds/m² over 24 h. Per-nest totals were not available in the record. [PR, other sp.] — [IOBC-WPRS abstract](https://iobc-wprs.org/?p=20771)
- A captive M. structor colony ate "one steppe cricket per day" in late winter while rearing brood. This is an anecdotal protein-intake data point. [HOBBY, captive] — [ameisenforum.de](https://ameisenforum.de/messor-structor-haltungserfahrungen-t24820-8.html)

### Inferences
- Order-of-magnitude placeholder for a sim: a mid-sized colony (about 1,000–3,000 workers) making about 2,000–4,000 successful trips per active day at about 1–10 mg per seed would bring in roughly 2–40 g of seed per good foraging day.
- Activity stops on cold, hot or rainy days, and essentially nothing is collected in winter.
- This is an extrapolation combining a New World trip rate with European seed masses. It should be tuned for gameplay, not presented as measured.

### Gaps
- No per-worker or per-colony daily seed-consumption measurements for any Old World Messor were found.
- The full texts of Baraibar/Westerman (J. Appl. Ecol. 2009, 46: 380–387) and Azcárate & Peco (2003, 2007) may contain per-nest removal rates but were not accessible.
